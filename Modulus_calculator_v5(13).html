Two-Point Method — calculates Young's modulus as the slope between two manually selected points on the stress-strain curve: E = (σ₂ − σ₁) / (ε₂ − ε₁) × 100. Fast and intuitive, but sensitive to point selection and measurement noise — results vary depending on which points are chosen. Best used for quick estimates. For reproducible results, prefer the Linear Regression tab.

Linear Regression Method — fits a line through all data points in the selected range by minimizing the sum of squared residuals (least squares). The slope gives E: σ = b · ε + a → E = b × 100. More reproducible and noise-resistant than two-point — random scatter in individual measurements averages out. Provides statistical confidence metrics (R², std error, 95% CI). Three methods available: OLS through origin (physically correct — no stress at zero strain), OLS free intercept (accounts for pre-load offset), Weighted (1/σ²) (down-weights high-stress outliers). Preferred for publication and reporting per ISO 6892.
